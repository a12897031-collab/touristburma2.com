## Executive Summary

During the reign of General Ne Win and the Burma Socialist Programme Party (BSPP), Burma (now Myanmar) engaged in one of the most drastic experiments in state isolation in modern history. Following the 1962 military coup, foreign influence was systematically curtailed, private enterprise was nationalized, and the nation turned inward under the state philosophy known as the **"Burmese Way to Socialism"** (*Lanzin*).

Yet, the state still required foreign currency. The government’s answer to controlled, highly monitored, and revenue-extracting foreign visitation was **Tourist Burma**—the official state-run travel corporation and tourism board.

---

## 1. Institutional Origins & Nationalization

Tourist Burma was originally created in **1962** under the umbrella of the **Burma Economic Development Corporation (BEDC)**, a military-controlled conglomerate created to streamline national strategic businesses.

### The Transition to Corporation No. 20
By **1964**, under the sweeping nationalization decrees of the Revolutionary Council, private travel agencies, foreign-owned hotels, and transportation companies were seized by the state. The BEDC's commercial assets were reorganized under government ministries:

* **Parent Entity:** Ministry of Trade
* **Operating Division:** Trade Corporation No. 20 (Hotel and Tourism Enterprise)
* **Public Brand Name:** **Tourist Burma**

Overnight, Tourist Burma became a **total monopoly**. It was illegal for any private citizen to act as a tour guide, operate a travel agency, rent rooms to foreign nationals, or transport tourists for commercial gain without government accreditation.

## 2. The 7-Day Visa & The "Standard Circuit"

Foreign policy under Ne Win was defined by non-alignment and paranoia regarding external intelligence services, foreign media, and political subversion. Visas were severely restricted:

1. **1960s:** 24-hour to 72-hour transit visas only.
2. **1970s–1980s:** Standardized to an absolute maximum **7-Day Tourist Visa** (168 hours).

```
=========================================================================
               UNION OF BURMA IMMIGRATION DEPARTMENT
                       TOURIST VISA - ENTRY
=========================================================================
  Visa No: TB-78904-RNG                   Date of Issue: 14 OCT 1978
  Bearer:  [FOREIGN NATIONAL]             Valid For: 7 DAYS ONLY
  Entry Port: RANGOON AIRPORT (MINGALADON)

  NOTICE TO BEARER: 
  - Overstaying is strictly prohibited under the Immigration Act.
  - Foreign currency must be declared upon arrival on Form C-102.
  - Accommodation limited exclusively to Tourist Burma approved facilities.
=========================================================================
```

Because of the strict 7-day limit, Tourist Burma funneled all visitors through a rigid, high-speed itinerary known to backpackers and travelers of the era as the **"Big Four" Loop**:

```
[ Rangoon ] ---> [ Mandalay ] ---> [ Pagan (Bagan) ] ---> [ Inle Lake ]
    ^                                                          |
    |                                                          |
    +------------------ ( Overnight Train/Flight ) <-----------+
```

1. **Rangoon (Yangon):** Entry port, exchange of currency, Shwedagon Pagoda, Sule Pagoda, and the Strand Hotel.
2. **Mandalay:** The last royal capital, Mahamuni Pagoda, Mandalay Hill.
3. **Pagan (Bagan):** The ancient city of thousands of Buddhist temples.
4. **Inle Lake:** Leg-rowing Intha fishermen and Phaung Daw Oo Pagoda.

## 3. State-Run Hospitality & Transport Infrastructure

### The Nationalized Strand Hotel
The crown jewel of Tourist Burma's hotel portfolio was **The Strand Hotel** on Strand Road in Rangoon. Once considered one of the finest colonial luxury hotels in Southeast Asia, under state management it entered a period of muted, nostalgic decay.

* **Atmosphere:** Worn teak flooring, antique ceiling fans rotating slowly, dim lighting, and elderly uniformed staff who had served since the pre-independence era.
* **Services:** Served as the official check-in point for foreign registration and currency conversion.

### Transport: Fleet and Rail Logistics
To move tourists along the approved routes, Tourist Burma maintained a fleet of imported Japanese vehicles—predominantly **Hino** and **Isuzu** buses—alongside state-operated railway cars attached to the Union of Burma Railways network.

* **Buses:** Air-conditioning was a rare luxury and frequently broken. Buses were painted with the Tourist Burma logo and carried government-assigned drivers and state-sanctioned guides.
* **Rail:** The overnight train from Rangoon to Mandalay was a 16-hour journey. Tourists were assigned specific upper-class carriages.
* **Domestic Flights:** Burma Airways Corporation (BAC) operated internal flights using Fokker F27 Friendship aircraft, though schedules were notoriously unreliable due to fuel shortages and maintenance issues.

## 5. Currency Control and the Black Market Economy

Under the BSPP, strict price controls and import bans created widespread shortages of consumer goods (electronics, cosmetics, medicine, foreign liquor, and cigarettes). This gave rise to Burma’s notorious **Black Market economy** (often called the *"Brown Economy"* or *"Kyat Market"*).

### The Tourist Experience on the Ground
1. **Official Rate vs. Black Market:** The official exchange rate set by the government was around **6.8 Kyats per US Dollar**. However, on the street, the black market rate ranged from **20 to 40 Kyats per US Dollar** (and higher by the late 1980s).
2. **The "Johnnie Walker & 555" Currency:** Tourists quickly realized that cartons of *State Express 555 cigarettes* and bottles of *Johnnie Walker Red Label* purchased at duty-free shops in Bangkok or Singapore were more valuable than cash. A single carton of 555 cigarettes sold on the black market could fund several days of travel inside the country.

## 6. End of an Era: The 1988 Uprising and Aftermath

The Tourist Burma model operated unchanged for over two decades. However, years of isolationist economic policy, demonetization decrees, and severe mis-management culminated in the **8888 Nationwide Popular Uprising** in August 1988.

Following the collapse of Ne Win’s BSPP regime and the assumption of power by the State Law and Order Restoration Council (SLORC):

* The BSPP state-monopoly system was dismantled.
* Tourist Burma was reorganized and eventually renamed the **Myanmar Hotels and Tourism Enterprise (MHTE)** under the newly created Ministry of Hotels and Tourism.
* The 7-day visa restriction was expanded to 14 days in 1992, and later to 28 days.
* Private tour companies, private hotels, and foreign hotel investments were re-permitted starting in the mid-1990s.

---

## Archival Conclusion

*Tourist Burma* remains a unique phenomenon in 20th-century Asian history: a state enterprise designed not to open a country up, but to keep it locked away while permitting just enough access to capture precious foreign reserves. For a generation of international travelers between 1962 and 1988, Tourist Burma was the sole, enigmatic gateway to a nation frozen in time.
